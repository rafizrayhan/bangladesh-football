How it works

The data

322 full men's internationals from July 1973 to 14 November 2026, from the open international results dataset on GitHub plus the recent matches it does not have. Two 2007 Merdeka Tournament games were played by the Bangladesh B team while the senior side was at the Nehru Cup, so they are removed. Every 2025 and 2026 match was checked against news reports, which found four the dataset lacks: the 1-0 defeat in Singapore on 31 March 2026 (the last Asian Cup qualifier) and Bangladesh's three games at the 2026 SAFF Championship in Dhaka: 1-0 v Bhutan on 5 November, 4-0 v Sri Lanka on 11 November and the 0-1 semi-final defeat to Pakistan on 14 November. The other teams' games at the tournament (India 3-2 Pakistan, Pakistan 3-0 Maldives, Sri Lanka 2-2 Bhutan, India 1-0 Maldives and the India 2-1 Bhutan semi-final) were added too, so the South Asia comparison covers every SAFF team to 14 November; the final, India v Pakistan on 17 November, does not involve Bangladesh and is not included. India sent a second-string senior squad, led by their under-23 coach, because their first team was playing friendlies in New Zealand; these were still full India internationals, so they count. Nepal (suspended by FIFA, not at the tournament) and Afghanistan run to the dataset's last update, 6 October 2026; the dataset also lacks the other 31 March 2026 Asian Cup qualifiers.

Cross-checks and corrections

The match list was checked year by year against the yearly totals in the World Football Elo archive, and disputed years against RSSSF's Bangladesh list and 11v11.com. A match was added or changed only when two independent sources agreed on date, opponent and score. Nine Bangladesh matches were added, scores given Bangladesh first: lost 0-2 to Bahrain and beat Sri Lanka 3-1 at the 1979 Korea Cup, lost 0-1 to North Korea at the 1985 Quaid-e-Azam Cup, beat Yemen 1-0 in Doha in 1994, lost 0-1 to India in the 2009 SAFF Cup semi-final, lost 0-1 in Singapore in 2026, and beat Bhutan 1-0 and Sri Lanka 4-0 and lost 0-1 to Pakistan at the 2026 SAFF Championship. The 1995 SAFF Cup win over Nepal was moved from 25 to 27 March. Seven years still differ from the Elo archive by a match or two, where no second source agreed; those were left as they are.

Elo ratings

Every international since 1872 is rated with the World Football Elo method: a World Cup match counts three times a friendly, wider margins count more, and the home side gets 100 points. Teams that joined later, like Bangladesh in 1973, start from the level they actually showed in their first 30 matches rather than a made-up average. A rating means something relative to other teams, not on its own.

Win, draw and loss chances

Fitted to every international since 1990 from the rating gap, with home advantage as a separate term (worth about 128 rating points; none on neutral ground). The model is symmetric, so on a neutral ground it does not matter which team is named first. They are well calibrated: teams given an 8% chance won 8% of the time, and teams given 90% won 91%. Chances are shown as whole percentages that add up to 100. "Points vs expected" counts 1 per win and ½ per draw.

Filters

Elo ratings follow the year filter only, because a team's strength does not change with "friendlies only". Every results figure follows all filters. Penalty shootouts count as draws.

Coaches and tournament fact sheets

Coach dates come from Wikipedia's list of Bangladesh managers. Where a coach left and the next arrived in the same month, the matches go to the outgoing coach. 57 of 68 spells match Wikipedia's own record exactly; the rest differ by a match or two because the sources list slightly different games.

The stage reached in each edition comes from Wikipedia's record tables for the World Cup, Asian Cup, SAFF Championship, South Asian Games, AFC Challenge Cup and Asian Games, and was checked edition by edition against the matches in this list (number of games, results, goals, and whether a final was played). Where the two differ, the fact sheet says so. Titles and runner-up finishes come from Wikipedia's honours list; each one whose matches are in this list was checked against them, and the three friendly events (Yangon 1995, the 2020 Mujib Borsho series, Kathmandu 2021) against a second source. Seven friendlies now carry their event's name in the match list. Joint World Cup and Asian Cup qualifiers are counted once, under World Cup qualifiers.

Records, regions and home advantage

Records and streaks follow every filter, and streaks count consecutive matches within the selection. Shootout wins and losses count as draws. Home advantage compares points won with points expected at home, away and on neutral grounds; a positive number at home means Bangladesh did better in Dhaka than even a home-adjusted model expected.

Record holders and the FIFA ranking

The open dataset names the scorer for only a small share of Bangladesh's goals, so the caps, goals and clean-sheet tables come from Wikipedia's lists. Matches since Wikipedia's 5 June 2026 update were checked in match reports to 14 November 2026: Rakib Hossain's goal against Sri Lanka was his seventh (level 9th), and the two clean sheets kept since, both by Mehedi Hasan Srabon, put him level 10th. Caps were cross-checked against two more sources. RSSSF's match-by-match record gives Jamal Bhuyan 94 caps to 5 June 2026; with his three SAFF Championship games that makes 97, which his Wikipedia page now also shows. National-Football-Teams.com agrees on the top three but differs by 1 to 8 caps for several older players, so places 4 to 10 of the caps list are approximate. The scorers named in the open dataset all fit within the goals list, and the clean-sheet totals were checked against the clean sheets in this match list, keeper by keeper and career by career. The highest and lowest FIFA ranks come from FIFA's Bangladesh ranking page and the Wikipedia infobox, which agree. The current rank (182nd, 7 October 2026, after the FIFA ASEAN Cup) comes from the Wikipedia infobox and the Daily Sun, because FIFA's page still showed the July ranking when checked.

What the data cannot show

There is no public event data for Bangladesh (shots, chances, passing), and the open dataset names the scorer for only goals, so apart from the record holders this dashboard is about results and strength, not tactics or individual players.
